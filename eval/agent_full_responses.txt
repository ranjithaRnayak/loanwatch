Q6: Within how many days must a Red Flagged Account be reported on CRILC?
Tool: Both | VQR: true | Clauses: FRM24:4.1.3, FRM24:3.3.4
Query ID: 01c78db6-0204-e005-0005-57520014b4ce | Elapsed: 15.6s
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Red Flagged Account must be reported on the Reserve Bank's CRILC platform **immediately — not later than seven days from the date of classification as a red-flagged account.**

Per FRM 2024, para 4.1.3: "The decision to classify any account, either standard or NPA, as a red-flagged account shall be at the individual bank level and such bank(s) shall report the status of the account on the Reserve Bank's CRILC platform immediately (not later than seven days from date of classification as red-flagged account)."

This CRILC reporting obligation applies to accounts meeting the CRILC threshold of Rs 3 crore aggregate exposure (FRM24:3.3.4).

Q15: How long does a bank have to decide whether an RFA is fraud?
Tool: Search | VQR: false | Clauses: FRAUD16:8.8.2, FRAUD16:8.9.4, FRM24:3.3.4
Query ID: 01c78dbd-0204-e117-0005-57520015100a | Elapsed: 19.9s
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A bank has a maximum of **six months** from red-flagging an account to take a final view — at the end of that timeline it must either lift the RFA status or classify the account as a fraud.

Per the 2016 Frauds Master Direction: "The bank may use external auditors, including forensic experts or an internal team for investigations before taking a final view on the RFA. At the end of this time line, which cannot be more than six months, banks should either lift the RFA status or classify the account as a fraud." (FRAUD16, para 8.8.2).

Related deadlines:
- If classified as fraud straightaway: report to RBI within 21 days, report to CBI/Police, request JLF meeting within 15 days (FRAUD16:8.9.4).
- CRILC reporting within 7 days of red-flagging (FRM24:3.3.4).

Q16: When can an NPA account be upgraded to standard?
Tool: Search | VQR: false | Clauses: IRACP25:69, IRACP25:71, IRACP25:72, IRACP25:62A
Query ID: 01c78dbe-0204-e117-0005-575200151016 | Elapsed: 16.9s
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
An NPA account can be upgraded to a standard asset only when the borrower has cleared all overdue amounts.

**General rule (IRACP25 para 69):** "The loan accounts classified as NPAs may be upgraded as 'standard' asset only if entire arrears of interest and principal are paid by the borrower."

**Multiple facilities (para 71):** All arrears across every facility must be cleared.

**Partial/technical write-offs (para 72):** Cannot show remaining part as standard.

**Resolution plans (para 62A):** Accounts that slipped into NPA due to a calamity may be upgraded upon resolution plan implementation.

Q17: What is the CTR threshold for cash transactions?
Tool: Search | VQR: false | Clauses: KYC25:PML_Rule3
Query ID: 01c78dbe-0204-df5c-0005-57520014f1fa | Elapsed: 13.5s
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Cash Transaction Report (CTR) threshold is **Rs. 10 lakh**.

Under PML Rules 2005 Rule 3, a CTR must be filed for:
(a) all cash transactions above Rs. 10 lakh (or equivalent in foreign currency), and
(b) all series of integrally connected cash transactions individually below Rs. 10 lakh that aggregate above Rs. 10 lakh within a month.

Structuring deposits just below Rs. 10 lakh (e.g. Rs. 9.4–9.9 lakh) to avoid a CTR is itself a suspicious activity and must be reported via an STR.

Clause: KYC25:PML_Rule3.

Q18: Is the 2016 Early Warning Signals list still in force?
Tool: Search | VQR: false | Clauses: FRM24:3.1.1, FRM24:3.3.3, FRAUD16:AnnexII
Query ID: 01c78dbf-0204-e117-0005-57520015101e | Elapsed: 22.0s
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
No. The 2016 Early Warning Signals list — Annex II of the Frauds Master Direction 2016 — is **illustrative and has been superseded**. The current, in-force framework is the RBI Fraud Risk Management Master Directions 2024 (FRM 2024).

Under FRM 2024, banks must maintain a Board-approved EWS and Red Flagging of Accounts (RFA) framework (para 3.1.1). The generation of an EWS alert triggers examination of whether the account should be red flagged (para 3.3.3).

The 2016 Annex II items remain only as an illustrative, superseded reference — they are not the governing list. For live monitoring rely on the FRM 2024 EWS/RFA framework.
